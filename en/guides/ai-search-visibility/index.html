AI Search Visibility: From Crawl Eligibility to Measured Mentions

A practical model for separating technical eligibility, retrieval evidence and neutral-prompt mention measurement.

01

Eligibility comes before mention tracking

A site that blocks relevant retrieval crawlers or exposes unstable public content may reduce its opportunity to be discovered. Technical access is therefore a prerequisite layer, not a mention metric.

02

Mentions and citations are different

A generated answer can mention a brand without citing its domain, and it can cite a domain without using the exact brand string. A serious tracker should measure those states separately.

03

Neutral prompts reduce self-injection

If the tracked brand or domain is included in the test prompt, the measurement is biased. Neutral prompts make the observation more useful by asking the provider to retrieve or generate without being handed the target entity.

04

API surfaces are not consumer-app replicas

Provider APIs and consumer products can use different models, retrieval policies or ranking layers. Results should therefore identify the measured surface and avoid claiming they exactly reproduce the consumer application.

05

Time series creates the real decision value

One observation is a snapshot. Repeating the same neutral prompt set under a stable measurement contract creates a trend that can be compared with technical changes, content releases or crawl-policy updates.
