The First Technical Gate for ChatGPT Search Visibility: OAI-SearchBot Access

A public website can appear in answers, but accidental crawler blocking can undermine discovery, snippets and clear citations.

2026-08-29CHATGPT DISCOVERY

What changed?

Public web content can be eligible to appear in ChatGPT Search. The most basic technical check is whether the search crawler can reach critical pages without being blocked by robots.txt or another access layer. Referral tagging also makes it possible to evaluate AI visibility through actual visits, not only brand mentions.

Why it matters to a website owner

Part of AI search visibility is a measurable access problem. Strong content cannot be evaluated normally if the crawler cannot read the critical URL.

Technical impact

robots.txt policy should distinguish search crawling, model-training crawling and user-agent actions. A blanket allow-every-AI-bot rule is less useful than reporting the site's deliberate policy choices.

What should be checked now?

  1. Verify OAI-SearchBot policy for critical public URLs.
  2. Do not confuse robots crawling controls with noindex intent.
  3. Measure AI referral traffic separately and connect it to conversion paths.
EVIDENCE BOUNDARY

Crawler access does not mean ChatGPT will recommend a brand or cite a page; it is one technical eligibility gate.
