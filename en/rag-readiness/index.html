Definition

A technique that retrieves relevant external information and uses it to ground a generated response.

What does Google say?

Google's 2026 generative AI Search guide explicitly describes retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), also called grounding, as a technique used with Search ranking systems to retrieve relevant up-to-date pages before generating responses.

What it means for AI systems

Retrieval works better when important information is textual, coherent, uniquely useful, linked and accessible at stable URLs.

Why it matters to a website owner

The site owner wants useful facts, products and expertise to be retrievable in the right context when an AI system answers a relevant question.

How HTML&HTML measures it

HTML&HTML evaluates oversized content blocks, context loss, duplication, self-contained sections, discovery paths and semantic integrity.

What it does not guarantee

RAG is a retrieval technique, not a standalone Google ranking factor and not a guarantee that a particular page will be cited.
